# PravixoEduTech Competitor UI & Feature Inspiration Lock

**Status:** Product inspiration reference
**Sources reviewed:** User-provided Testbook and Oliveboard mobile screenshots
**Purpose:** Preserve product, information-architecture, UX, monetization, mock-test, personalization, and future AI inspiration without copying exact UI, text, branding, assets, layouts, or proprietary flows.

## 1. Non-copying rule

PravixoEduTech must not reproduce either competitor's exact screen layouts, navigation order, colors, icons, illustrations, banners, promotional copy, plan names, card compositions, labels, or proprietary interaction flows.

The correct approach is to identify the underlying student need and design a distinct Pravixo solution that is simpler, more trustworthy, more Hindi-first, and more connected across recruitment, exams, content, PYQs, mock tests, analytics, and personalized study.

## 2. Testbook inspiration

### Product pattern observed

Testbook presents an all-in-one preparation ecosystem with:
- an all-exams subscription;
- mock tests and test series;
- PYPs/PYQs;
- study notes;
- current affairs and news;
- coaching/super packages;
- books;
- enrolled exams;
- notifications;
- library/bookmarks;
- referrals;
- AI mentor and doubt assistant;
- progress indicators;
- profile and account settings.

### Strong ideas worth learning from

#### My Exams personalization
Users can select multiple exams and later manage their enrolled exams. This can influence home recommendations, tests, notes, PYQs, alerts, courses, and news.

**Pravixo lesson:** exam selection should become a first-class personalization layer.

#### Broad exam discovery
High-level categories are clearly visible, including SSC, Railways, Banking, Teaching, Civil Services, State Exams, Police, Insurance, Defence, Nursing, Judiciary, Regulatory Bodies, entrance exams, and private-job skills.

**Pravixo lesson:** use a clear Exam Group → Exam hierarchy while keeping government exams as the core identity.

#### Persistent selected-exam state
Selected exams remain visible and affect the user experience.

**Pravixo lesson:** persist student exam preferences and use them consistently throughout the platform.

#### Subscription transparency
The pass screen communicates what is included, how much validity remains, coverage, price, and renewal.

**Pravixo lesson:** entitlement and expiry information should be explicit and easy to verify.

#### Series progress
Test-series cards display attempted/total progress.

**Pravixo lesson:** every series should show completed, attempted, remaining, last activity, and the recommended next action.

#### AI mentor entry points
The AI area suggests study plans, doubt solving, mock practice, prompt suggestions, chat history, and progress data.

**Pravixo lesson:** AI should be exam-aware and grounded in trusted Pravixo and official-source data.

#### Profile and settings structure
The app separates profile, language, theme, support, transactions, subscriptions, notifications, referrals, and saved content.

**Pravixo lesson:** preserve these as future account capabilities, but present them in a cleaner structure.

### Weaknesses to avoid

- excessive commercialization and overlapping pass types;
- crowded global navigation;
- too many selected exams diluting personalization;
- repetitive category cards;
- AI without visible source grounding;
- banner-heavy screens.

**Pravixo response:** use restrained monetization, a stable information architecture, a primary-exam concept, strong entity separation, source-aware AI, and fewer promotional surfaces.

## 3. Oliveboard inspiration

### Product pattern observed

Oliveboard appears more exam-category and test-structure focused. The screenshots show:
- sign-up;
- primary exam-category selection;
- category-specific home;
- tests;
- previous-year papers;
- courses;
- toppers and success stories;
- store/subscriptions;
- bookmarks;
- notes and videos;
- current affairs;
- source/disclaimer pages;
- night mode.

### Strong ideas worth learning from

#### Primary category onboarding
The onboarding asks users to choose a primary exam category while allowing access to others.

**Pravixo lesson:** ask for preferred language, primary exam, preparation stage, target attempt/date, and daily availability. Keep it brief and skippable.

#### Category-to-exam hierarchy
Exams are grouped under larger categories such as SSC, Railway, Banking, Regulatory, Civil Services, state exams, judiciary, agriculture, and MBA.

**Pravixo lesson:** maintain separate concepts for Exam Group, Organization, Exam, and Recruitment. TaxonomyNode remains academic Subject/Topic/Subtopic, not exam grouping.

#### Dedicated PYQ navigation
PYQs are separated from regular tests and organized by exam and tier.

**Pravixo lesson:** PYQs should be first-class, with Exam, Stage, Year, Shift, Paper, Attempt, Solution, and Topic Analysis.

#### Exam-stage segmentation
The screenshots distinguish Tier I, Tier II, Stage 2, typing tests, and descriptive variants.

**Pravixo lesson:** the Exam → Stage → ExamPattern relationship is essential and should drive syllabus, PYQs, notifications, mock tests, and analytics.

#### Feature comparison
Plans communicate included and excluded features.

**Pravixo lesson:** use a compact structured comparison matrix rather than very long scrolling text.

#### Utility features
Bookmarks, saved notes, saved videos, current affairs, subscriptions, and payments are separate utilities.

**Pravixo lesson:** future bookmarks should work consistently across Content, Exam, Recruitment, MockTest, Question, and Course.

#### Source/disclaimer visibility
The presence of a source/disclaimer area signals trust awareness.

**Pravixo lesson:** go further by showing official source, source date, verification date, reviewer, correction history, and status directly on relevant pages.

### Weaknesses to avoid

- dense long-form plan pages;
- promotional countdowns dominating navigation;
- repetitive static exam grids;
- older dense visual patterns;
- personalization only at broad category level.

**Pravixo response:** use concise plan cards, real deadline countdowns only, search plus personalized discovery, modern accessible spacing, and exact Exam/Stage personalization.

## 5. Pravixo differentiation

Pravixo should not try to win through more banners, plan names, or menu items. It should win through a connected, trustworthy, Hindi-first preparation system.

### Connected opportunity-to-preparation journey

Recruitment → Exam → Stage → Pattern → Syllabus → Notes → PYQs → Mock Tests → Results → Weak Topics → Revision Plan

This complete chain should become a core Pravixo advantage.

### Hindi-first but structurally bilingual

Support:
- Hindi and English entity names;
- language-specific Content records;
- bilingual Questions and Groups;
- bilingual search aliases;
- language-aware recommendations;
- language-aware AI responses.

### Trust-first public information

Every recruitment, exam update, admit card, result, and syllabus page should show:
- official source;
- status;
- published date;
- updated date;
- last verified date;
- author/reviewer;
- correction history;
- recruitment timeline.

### Topic intelligence

TaxonomyNode should eventually support, per Subject/Topic/Subtopic:
- syllabus relevance;
- notes;
- current affairs;
- PYQs;
- practice questions;
- mock-test coverage;
- student accuracy;
- time spent;
- mastery;
- recommended next activity.

### Transparent monetization

Plans should explain exact Exams included, test types, attempt limits, validity, downloads, language support, renewal behavior, refund policy, and true final price. Avoid fake scarcity, hidden renewal, and confusing overlapping passes.

## 7. Pravixo UI principles

### Mobile-first hierarchy
Each screen should have one primary purpose, one main CTA, a clear title, visible Exam/Stage context, minimal promotion, accessible tap targets, and readable Hindi/English typography.

### Progressive disclosure
A Mock Test card should initially show:
- title;
- Exam and Stage;
- full/sectional/topic/PYQ type;
- question count;
- duration;
- access state;
- progress;
- primary action.

Secondary information should open in details.

### Stable navigation
Public navigation remains:
- Home;
- Exams;
- Study;
- Current Affairs;
- Jobs & Updates;
- Mock Tests;
- Courses.

Student dashboard shortcuts should be personalized rather than permanently competing in the global navigation.

### Distinct visual identity
Pravixo must use its own colors, typography, icon set, card composition, illustrations, motion, and copywriting voice. The target is calm, credible, modern, Hindi-friendly, and less commercial-looking than the references.

## 8. How TaxonomyNode supports this direction

TaxonomyNode enables:

### Exam and content connection
Exam → Subjects → Topics → Notes/PYQs/Questions/Mocks

### Test discovery
Users can browse full-length tests, section tests, Subject tests, Topic tests, Subtopic practice, and PYQs by taxonomy coverage.

### Analytics
Student performance can be aggregated by stable IDs:
- Subject accuracy;
- Topic accuracy;
- Subtopic weakness;
- time spent;
- attempts;
- mastery.

### AI grounding
The AI mentor can understand the exact target Exam, its Subjects, weak Topics, related verified content, available tests, and recent progress.

### Recommendations
The system can recommend a note before a test, a PYQ after a weak result, a Topic test after revision, or a full mock when readiness improves.

## 10. Launch-scope override — 29 July 2026

Until the first production launch, budget, speed, security, stability, and core usability take priority over feature breadth.

### Launch priorities

1. Public website for trusted exam content and paid/free notes.
2. Student dashboard for login-protected Mock Tests, attempts, results, review, history, and paid notes.
3. Admin dashboard for creating and editing public content and managing Mock Tests.
4. Referral workflows already substantially developed, because they are important for marketing and sales.
5. Payments and entitlement flows already substantially developed.
6. Strong mobile UI, security, tenant isolation, production stability, and launch testing across all of the above.

### Deferred until after launch

- AI mentor, doubt solving, adaptive learning, and generated study plans;
- LMS, live classes, and advanced course delivery;
- advanced recommendations, mastery forecasting, and deep gamification;
- broad competitor-inspired feature expansion;
- optional role-management enhancements that are not required for secure launch;
- expensive infrastructure or features without clear launch value.

### Scope-control rule

A feature may enter the pre-launch plan only when it:

- blocks the core launch journey;
- materially improves competitiveness or trust;
- is affordable to build and operate;
- can be fully tested before launch;
- does not destabilize completed modules.

Otherwise it must remain documented for a post-launch phase.

## 12. Durable product rule

For every future feature, ask:

1. Which student problem does it solve?
2. Which Pravixo entity owns the data?
3. Is it connected to Exam and taxonomy?
4. Does it work in Hindi and English?
5. Is it trustworthy and source-aware?
6. Is it simpler than the competitor pattern?
7. Can it be administered safely?
8. Does it preserve tenant isolation?
9. Does it improve preparation outcomes rather than only engagement?
10. Is the design original?

This document is an inspiration lock, not a competitor-copy specification.
